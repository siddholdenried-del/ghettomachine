A part is one track's note or hit data in one pattern. There are 16 patterns, four parts per pattern, and up to 128 steps per part. A scene normally launches all four parts together; HOME can also launch each track's clip separately.

Pick and edit a drum

On DRUMS, PRESETS picks 909, 808, 606, 707 or a user kit. Tap a black key to both audition and select its pad. SEQ shows that pad's steps; EDIT shows its sound knobs. Press EDIT again, or use SELECT, for further parameter pages.

On the 606 kit, black keys 4, 6 and 11 are silent, and key 10 is CY. Your steps are retained when switching kits. The parameters depend on the pad and kit. Start with tune and decay; try a little reverb send on the clap before turning up reverb for the whole drum track. The last EDIT page exposes kit-wide controls. ENV on DRUMS opens pad MUTE: black keys mute individual pads in this pattern, and OCT+ unmutes them all.

Mix models without moving the steps

Pick a base kit with PRESETS. Tap the black key for the lane you want, then open EDIT and turn SELECT to the last page, DRUM MODEL. Turn K1 to choose the model. This changes the voice on that lane, not its steps, mute or MIDI note. The 707 kit and aux voices are synthesized, not sampled.

Choose 707 COWBELL or 707 TAMBOURINE to replace a voice on an existing lane. Each can occupy one lane at a time; neither adds a lane. Hold SAVE and turn PRESETS to keep a mixed kit in a user slot, then release SAVE. For a stock kit again, select a factory kit with PRESETS.

Steps, banks, length and rate

The four knobs on STEPS with no step held
KnobMeaningWhat it affects
K1 LENGTH1–128 steps.Just this track's current part. Set all four the same for a regular loop, or try a 15-step hat part against a 16-step bass.
K2 RATE1/16, 1/8, 1/32, 1/16T or 1/8T.Just this part. T is a triplet division; 16 steps at 1/16 is a bar in 4/4.
K3 SWING50–75%; 50% is straight.The project groove, not a per-step timing offset.
K4 TEMPO60–240 BPM.The shared clock; MIDI FOLLOW takes its tempo from the incoming source.

Each bank displays 16 steps: from steps 1–16 through 113–128. On DRUMS the keys of the beats, steps 1, 5, 9 and 13 of the bank shown, glow dimly while they're empty, and the screen shades their columns, so a bar is easy to count. A step with a hit stays fully lit. Raise LENGTH before looking for a higher bank. On DRUMS, move banks with OCT−/OCT+ or SELECT. On instrument STEPS, black keys 1–8 go straight to banks that exist at the current length. Those same black keys play notes on KEYS.

A quick tap on an empty step lights it. A quick tap and release on an already lit step clears it and its locks. To edit an existing step, keep holding it while you operate a secondary control; simply letting go without an edit can erase it. EDIT + OCT− undoes an accidental clear.

Hold SEQ and tap OCT+ to repeat the active part into a second half, with its notes, accents, chance and locks. SEQ + OCT− keeps the first half (rounds odd lengths down), removing the rest. The track’s rate stays the same. Doubling is blocked if the full copy would exceed 128 steps or the lock limit. EDIT + OCT− undoes the change; EDIT + OCT+ redoes.

On instrument STEPS, hold a step and press ARP to enter PICK NOTES. Tap keys to add or remove pitches (up to four), use OCT−/OCT+ to shift the picker’s range, and press ARP or SEQ to return. An empty step can also stamp the chord and inversion selected on ARP 2.

On instrument STEPS, hold a step and turn K3 GATE. Slow turns move by 1/8 step; fast turns snap to bars at the selected division. The maximum hold is 128 steps.

A lock sets one sound parameter for one step. Think of it as one hit's special setting, not a change to the base sound. A low-chance hit does not fire on every pass; the numbered steps are still in the pattern. Try putting chance on an extra percussion hit, not on your only kick. A good first lock is more decay on one closed hat.

Erase steps, parts and whole patterns

REC + GLO erases one track's part at once. There's no single button for a whole pattern. These are the fastest ways to erase, from one step up to a whole pattern. All of them can be undone with EDIT + OCT−.

If every one of the 16 patterns has something in it, there's no empty pattern to copy. Empty one first with the step and ACID methods above. Undo keeps the last eight pattern edits: steps, accents, locks, chance, pad mutes, lengths, rates, recordings, ACID lines and pattern copies. Each undo puts one whole pattern back as it was. It doesn't undo sound, mixer or effect changes; use SAVE + OCT− to reload the selected track's saved sound.

Write and edit an acid line

On T2–T4, cycle SEQ until ACID. This generator writes into the current part. It works with MONO, POLY or SAMPLER; it isn't a separate track. Copy the pattern first if it contains notes you want to keep.

  1. Try the ACID preset, then use SELECT to reach NOTES and choose a root and scale. Minor, Phrygian, Dorian, pentatonic, blues, harmonic minor, major and Mixolydian are available.
  2. Return to ACID with SELECT. Use moderate DENSITY, then add a little SLIDE and ACCENT. OCT+ makes a new line; OCT− changes only a few steps. Lowering and then restoring a knob gives the same line back for the same seed. Turning DENSITY up adds notes rather than shuffling all the old ones.
  3. Press SEQ through KEYS and back to STEPS if you want to edit the generated notes by hand. A half note is short, a whole note holds toward the next note, and a slide glides into the next without a fresh attack. GLIDE at zero uses the generator's roughly 58 ms slide. Accents give velocity 127 plus a quick filter lift; ordinary generated notes use velocity 90.
  4. Save when happy. Turning ACID controls again regenerates the part and replaces hand edits within the current length. Undo restores both line settings and steps. Patterns save and copy their generator state; a line of up to 128 steps is generated for the whole part, not tiled from 16.

The generator is for instrument tracks, not drums. The arpeggiator plays its own stream of notes and ignores the generated slides.
