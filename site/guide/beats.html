A part is one track's note or hit data in one pattern. There are 16 patterns, four parts per pattern, and up to 128 steps per part. A scene normally launches all four parts together; HOME can also launch each track's clip separately.

Pick and edit a drum

On DRUM STEPS, turn PRESETS to choose a genre kit, such as ELECTRO, HOUSE or TECHNO. Tap a black key to select and audition a pad. The white keys show that pad's steps. Changing kits or pad presets does not rewrite those steps.

To mute a lane without deleting its hits, press ENV and tap its black key. OCT+ unmutes all lanes. This mutes the audio, so a tail still ringing can come back as soon as you unmute. The Quick Mixer has the same mutes on its white keys.

Change one pad without moving its steps

Pick a pad, press EDIT, then turn PRESETS to change that pad only. Turn SELECT to move through its pages and use the four knobs to shape the sound. Release any held step before turning a sound knob. The Cymbal pad offers CRASH, RIDE, RISER and NOISE DROP; both Perc pads have ZAP, SYNC and RING models alongside their other modes.

On the hardware, project SAVE keeps the edited drums. SAVE + PRESETS does not yet save a reusable custom drum kit. Browser project saves last only until reload.

Steps, banks, length and rate

The four knobs on STEPS with no step held

Each bank displays 16 steps: from steps 1–16 through 113–128. On DRUMS the keys of the beats, steps 1, 5, 9 and 13 of the bank shown, glow dimly while they're empty, and the screen shades their columns, so a bar is easy to count. A step with a hit stays fully lit. Raise LENGTH before looking for a higher bank. On DRUMS, move banks with OCT−/OCT+. SELECT switches between PART GROOVE and DRUM HIT. On instrument STEPS, select PART GROOVE to reach LENGTH, RATE, SWING and TEMPO. On instrument STEPS, black keys 1–8 go straight to banks that exist at the current length. Those same black keys play notes on KEYS.

A quick tap on an empty step lights it. A quick tap and release on an already lit step clears it and its locks. To edit an existing step, keep holding it while you operate a secondary control; simply letting go without an edit can erase it. EDIT + OCT− undoes an accidental clear.

Hold SEQ and tap OCT+ to repeat the selected track’s sequence into a second half. SEQ + OCT− keeps the first half (rounds odd lengths down), removing the rest. The track’s rate stays the same. In the browser, Shift-click SEQ to hold it, click OCT+ once, then click SEQ again to release it. Doubling stops at the 128-step limit. In the format 14 build, if some step locks won’t fit, the steps still double and DOUBLED, BUT shows how many locks didn’t copy. Drum chance uses a lock, so check the copied half if you see that message. Format 12 instead refuses the double when the copied locks won’t fit. EDIT + OCT− undoes the change; EDIT + OCT+ redoes.

On instrument STEPS, hold a step and press ARP to enter PICK NOTES. Tap keys to add or remove pitches (up to four), use OCT−/OCT+ to shift the picker’s range, and press ARP or SEQ to return. An empty step can also stamp the chord and inversion selected on ARP 2.

On instrument STEPS, hold a step and turn K3 GATE. Slow turns move by 1/8 step; fast turns snap to bars at the selected division. The maximum hold is 128 steps.

Velocity, chance, substeps and accents

On DRUM STEPS, hold a lit step on the selected pad. The four knobs become K1 VELOCITY (1–127), K2 CHANCE (5–100%), K3 SUBSTEPS (1–4 hits), and K4 ACCENT (on/off for the whole step, across pads). Without a velocity edit, the hit plays at its normal or accented level. Set substeps to 1 for a single hit. Release the white key after making the edit.

On a synth's STEPS page, use SELECT to choose STEP NOTES for note, velocity, gate and chance, or STEP HARMONY for substeps and chord tools. Hold the step while editing. In STEP NOTES, turning K2 past 127 sets an accent. Use OCT+ with a synth step held for HALF, WHOLE or SLIDE.

Microtiming: Hold a step and turn PRESETS to push it later, up to 15/16 of the step. Turn back to ON TIME to remove the offset. Hold the step and press OCT− to clear its locks. Velocity, chance, substeps and nudge use lock slots where applicable.

Copy a step, drum lane or part

SAVE copies. REC pastes. Hold a white step to copy or paste that step (all pads in a drum column), hold a black pad key on Drum Steps for a whole drum lane, or hold SEQ for the selected track's whole part. At the destination, hold the matching control and press REC. The clipboard points to the source, so it pastes what's there at that moment. A drum part cannot paste onto a synth track. EDIT + OCT− undoes a paste.

Make variations with BEAT

Press SEQ on Drum Steps to open BEAT. Turn SELECT for MORE, LINE and KEEP. Use OCT+ for a new seed, OCT− for a smaller mutation, or MORE → K4 ROLLS to add closed-hat ratchets. In KEEP, K1 picks a lane and K2 protects it. Keep the kick and snare you like, then roll the hats again. BEAT writes ordinary hits into the pattern and replaces unprotected hits; use Undo if you prefer the old beat.

Erase steps, parts and whole patterns

REC + GLO erases one track's part at once. There's no single button for a whole pattern. These are the fastest ways to erase, from one step up to a whole pattern. All of them can be undone with EDIT + OCT−.

If every one of the 16 patterns has something in it, there's no empty pattern to copy. Empty one first with the step and ACID methods above. The format 14 build keeps up to five pattern edits (one on the FM-1 while Bluetooth audio is active): steps, accents, locks, chance, pad mutes, lengths, rates, recordings, ACID lines and pattern copies. Each undo puts one whole pattern back as it was. It doesn't undo sound, mixer or effect changes; use SAVE + OCT− to reload the selected track's saved sound.

Write and edit an acid line

On T2–T4, cycle SEQ until ACID. This generator writes into the current part. It works with MONO, POLY or SAMPLER; it isn't a separate track. Copy the pattern first if it contains notes you want to keep.

  1. Try the ACID preset, then use SELECT to reach NOTES and choose a root and scale. Minor, Phrygian, Dorian, pentatonic, blues, harmonic minor, major and Mixolydian are available.
  2. Return to ACID with SELECT. Use moderate DENSITY, then add a little SLIDE and ACCENT. OCT+ rolls the dice: each press picks a random seed out of 65,535, so you never walk through the same lines in the same order. OCT− changes only a few steps. To browse lines in order instead, turn K1 SEED on the LINE page: it steps up or down from the seed you're on, and turning back returns the line you had. EDIT + OCT− brings back the line before the last roll. Lowering and then restoring a knob gives the same line back for the same seed. Turning DENSITY up adds notes rather than shuffling all the old ones.
  3. Press SEQ through KEYS and back to STEPS if you want to edit the generated notes by hand. A half note is short, a whole note holds toward the next note, and a slide glides into the next without a fresh attack. GLIDE at zero uses the generator's roughly 58 ms slide. Accents give velocity 127 plus a quick filter lift; ordinary generated notes use velocity 90.
  4. Save when happy. Turning ACID controls again regenerates the part and replaces hand edits within the current length. Undo restores both line settings and steps. Patterns save and copy their generator state; a line of up to 128 steps is generated for the whole part, not tiled from 16.

The generator is for instrument tracks, not drums. The arpeggiator plays its own stream of notes and ignores the generated slides.
