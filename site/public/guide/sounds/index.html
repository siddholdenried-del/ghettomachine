Synths, samples and acid

T2, T3 and T4 each have their own sound. Start with a preset, make one change you can hear, then save it into a user slot when it earns a place in another pattern.

Choose MONO, POLY or SAMPLER

Turn ALGORITHM to an instrument track. In EDIT → VOICE, TYPE chooses the engine. LEVEL sets the sound's level before the mixer. PRESETS selects a whole sound, not merely a waveform.

Voice modes and what you can play
ModeUse it forImportant controls
MONOBass, leads and single-note lines.UNISON 1–4 stacks voices per note. GLIDE controls transition time. With held keys, releasing the newest note returns to an older one that is still held.
POLYChords, pads and overlapping tails.Up to eight voices per track, within a 16-voice pool shared by the instrument tracks. Release and unison can use up that pool quickly.
SAMPLERA recorded sound or imported WAV played at different pitches.Set trim, loop, root and pitch under EDIT. FILTER, ENV and the shaper can still shape the sound. See Sampling.

LEGATO on EDIT → MORE: OFF starts the envelopes for each note; ON keeps tied envelopes and allows GLIDE; SLIDE glides only tied notes. On an acid part, the generator's explicit slide marks can glide without a new attack regardless of the LEGATO setting.

Set an instrument insert

On T2, T3 or T4, hold SEL and tap FX. SELECT chooses Phaser, Trem / Pan, Chorus, Flanger, Comb, Env Filter, Freq Shift, Micro Pitch, Resonator or OFF. K1–K3 shape the selected mode and K4 MIX sets the wet level. Zero mix bypasses the insert without losing its other knob values. Tap FX to go back.

One insert per track. The insert is before the pan, USB stem and the shared reverb and delay sends. Muting the track cuts its insert tail; the already-ringing shared effects can continue. Settings are saved with the project, and older projects load with inserts off.

Find each sound parameter

Press EDIT repeatedly, or use SELECT while on its pages. The four cards on screen correspond to K1–K4. The sampler has its own sample pages instead of the normal oscillator pages.

ENV opens ADSR for the amp; then the filter; then MOD ENV. The filter envelope is audible when FILTER → ENV AMT is away from zero. The third envelope has no job until you route it in the matrix.

Hear what the advanced controls do

  1. Subtractive bass. Pick MONO and a saw or square. Lower CUTOFF, use moderate RESO, and set a positive filter ENV AMT. Shorten filter decay to get a quick edge without keeping the top end bright for the full note. Set a little SUB, then compare at matched level.
  2. FM bell. Pick sine for both oscillators. Turn OSC MIX to zero so only OSC 1 is directly audible. Bring up OSC 2 → FM and try INTERVAL +12 (2:1) or +19 (about 3:1). For a bell that softens after the strike, route a short-decay MOD ENV to FM. For a ready-made version to compare, load FM BELL.
  3. Shaped lead. Try FOLD on a simple waveform and raise AMOUNT slowly. PRE FLT lets a low-pass filter tame the harmonics afterward; POST FLT puts them back on the filtered sound. BIAS changes the symmetry. CLIP squares peaks, RECT flips the lower half of the wave, and CRUSH reduces resolution and holds samples.
  4. Slow pad. Start with a POLY preset, lengthen the amp attack and release a little, and add a small amount of DRIFT. For a vibrato that emerges after the note, route LFO 2 to PITCH at a small amount and increase FADE IN. If chords start losing voices, shorten release before adding more motion.

FM phase-modulates OSC 1 with OSC 2. RING moves toward multiplying the two waveforms. Both can get bright quickly. WAVE 2, FM and RING apply when OSC 1 is saw through PWM; SYNC and SUB use their own pairs.

LFOs, the matrix and macros

Which modulation page to open
SectionWhat it controlsWhen to use it
LFO page 1SHAPE, RATE, DEPTH, DEST.One LFO per track. On DRUMS, select a pad and pick one of its parameters as DEST. Sample-and-hold on closed-hat decay is a useful starting point.
LFO page 2SYNC, RETRIG, FADE IN, PHASE.Tempo sync is available from 4 bars to 1/32. RETRIG can run FREE or restart at NOTE or BAR.
LFO page 3 on T2–T4LFO 2 SHAPE, RATE, FADE IN, RESTART.Each note has its own LFO. Choose EACH NOTE or FREE for a rate in Hz, or NOTE SYNC or FREE SYNC to pick a tempo division. See LFO 2.
LFO page 4 on T2–T4Six MATRIX slots: slot, source, destination, signed amount.Route envelopes, velocity, note pitch, random or macros. An amount of zero is no modulation.

In MATRIX, K1 picks the slot. Set SOURCE, DEST and AMOUNT (−64 to +63). Destinations include PITCH, OSC 2, DETUNE, CUTOFF, RESO, PWM, MIX, SUB, NOISE, DRIVE, FM, RING, SHAPER, BIAS, LEVEL, PAN and LFO 2 RATE. Use two slots from MACRO 1, one to CUTOFF and one to SHAPER, then move EDIT → MACRO's first knob. You can lock or record macro moves and matrix amounts, but not LFO 1 or arp settings. The matrix cannot modulate another slot's amount. Modulation in depth has every source and destination with its range, how far each moves, and recipes.

Chords and arpeggios

ARP page 1 sets the order, timing division, octave span and gate. Page 2 has LATCH plus K2 CHORD and K3 INVERT. Try MIN on a POLY sound and play one key, then try 1ST inversion before recording. Available chord shapes include MAJ, MIN, dominant 7, MAJ7, MIN7, SUS2, SUS4, DIM, AUG, fifth and octave, plus four-note MIN9, MAJ9, 7#9 and 7SUS4 shapes. Chords record as up to four notes per step. The built-in keys expand to chords; external MIDI notes arrive as played. The arp can play held or sequenced chords, but does not write the resulting arpeggio back into the steps.

Jump between synth banks

On T2–T4, hold SEL and turn PRESETS to jump to a bank. Release SEL and turn PRESETS alone to audition individual sounds. The screen names the chosen bank and its first patch. The order is Original, User Sounds (only if a slot is used), Motor City, After Hours, Machine Funk, Warehouse and Dub / Broken.

Motor City adds 21 patches, and the four other banks add 21 each. Some new patches recall light Color on the selected track. Clean patches bypass it without deleting existing module settings. Original, Motor City and User patches do not change the track’s current Color. Saving a user sound saves the synth patch, not its Color settings.

Keep a sound without changing your project template

PRESETS retains the 24 original factory sounds and USER 1–16, followed by the five factory expansion banks. For drum presets, see Drums & steps. A star after the preset name means it's been edited. To save, hold SAVE, turn PRESETS to select a slot, check whether it says EMPTY or REPLACE, and release SAVE. Stop the transport first: while playing it says STOP FIRST. This immediately writes the user sound or entire kit separately from the project. Tap SAVE separately to keep the current project. Learn how presets and project saves differ.
